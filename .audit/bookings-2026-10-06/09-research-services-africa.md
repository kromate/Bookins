## 3. How leaders implement the notable ones
- Acuity tiering by calendars and revenue features: Starter 1 calendar; Standard unlocks SMS, waitlist, packages/memberships/gift certificates; Premium unlocks 36 calendars, AI chatbot, HIPAA BAA, CSS/API, multi-timezone. Intake forms are attached per appointment type and answered at booking (consent/health history). Classes carry attendee limits and waitlists.
- Setmore: generous free tier (4 users) funded by upselling SMS credits (500/mo), 2-way sync, deposits, recurring and branding removal on $5-12/user. Group classes: duration, price, seats, host, repeat daily/weekly/monthly.
- SimplyBook.me: feature-as-plugin model (packages, memberships, gift cards, loyalty, group bookings, recurring batches, intake forms, reviews, class booking, multi-location, POS) with a cap on count per plan; SMS and WhatsApp as prepaid credits ($8/100); no per-transaction fee; 14+ payment processors (Stripe, PayPal, Square, Adyen, Mollie, etc.; no Paystack/Flutterwave/M-Pesa found); multilingual.
- Fresha: marketplace + POS; revenue from subscription, payment processing, metered WhatsApp/SMS/email and a one-time new-client marketplace fee. Local pricing in NGN is shown, i.e. it prices for Nigeria; SMS marketing at NGN 2,663/message is effectively unusable for small salons.
- Booksy: single $29.99 plan with $20/staff, 2,000 free SMS, Boost = commission on first visit of a marketplace-sourced client (30%); Stripe-based payments and readers.
- Vagaro: low base + per-calendar seat; includes loyalty, inventory, forms, reminders; payments tied to its processor; extensive multi-location enterprise (flagship sync, payroll, API).
- Square Appointments: free for solo with Square processing; no-show fee/cancellation policy and waitlist at Plus; resources at Premium. Hard-tied to Square's processing, so unavailable in Africa.
- Booklink (ZA): minimal: shareable link, services, team, local gateways, WhatsApp/email reminders, Google Calendar; R79/mo Pro. Closest comparable to Bookins.
- WhatsApp Business Platform: since 1 Jul 2025 per-message pricing; utility templates are free inside the 24h customer-service window, charged outside; marketing templates always charged (official: https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing). Implication: a booking confirmation sent as a reply inside a customer-initiated chat is free; reminders a day later are paid.

## 4. Africa-specific market needs, with evidence
- WhatsApp-first: DataReportal (Nigeria, Digital 2026): 109M internet users (45.5%), 165M cellular connections; a Statista summary says WhatsApp is the most-used platform (over 95% of internet users 16+). Booklink, Saysimple (Paystack-in-WhatsApp links), Zoko, Interakt-type vendors all position WhatsApp as primary. Global leaders mostly sell email/SMS; only SimplyBook (credits) and Fresha (metered) offer WhatsApp. A WhatsApp click-to-chat / share-confirmation-text deep link needs no API cost.
- Mobile money: GSMA 2026: 2.3B registered accounts globally; SSA+North Africa 1.2B; Africa 347M 30-day active; SSA is 66% of global transaction value; $1.4T in SSA in 2025 (Connecting Africa headline). None of the global tools reviewed lists M-Pesa/MTN MoMo/Airtel Money natively. Kolonell (vendor, not independent) claims M-Pesa deposits sharply cut no-shows (KES 500 deposit "almost always shows up"; 25% no-show baseline -> ~7% with reminders; 30% deposit + WhatsApp -> 8%). Treat as marketing claims.
- Paystack / Flutterwave: WordPress plugins (BookingPress, Booknetic, Salon Booking System) sell Paystack add-ons, evidence of demand and of the gap in SaaS tools. Booklink and Saysimple integrate Paystack. Pricing: Fresha shows NGN pricing; Booklink R79; Kolonell KES 3,000-9,000/mo; francophone SaaS 12,000 FCFA/mo cited by Kolonell.
- Currency: local price display (NGN/KES/GHS/ZAR/XOF) is table stakes; Stripe/Square-only tools exclude most of Africa (Square card processing only in 8 countries; Stripe availability in NG/KE/GH unverified).
- Data cost and bandwidth: A4AI "1 for 2" target (1GB <= 2% income); one Nigeria analysis puts 1GB at ~6% of (old) minimum wage; 8 of 10 most expensive countries for 1GB in 2020 were African (older data). Implication: light pages, no heavy images/fonts, resilient to flaky networks, offline-tolerant owner view.
- Mobile-first: Fresha, Self Vibe and YoowiX all lead with Android/iOS apps; Setmore and Acuity have apps. A PWA/mobile-web booking page is cheaper to install-free for guests.
- Cash-at-venue norm: Kolonell highlights front-desk booking for the "digital divide"; offering "pay at venue" with optional deposit is likely standard. Unverified beyond vendor claims.
- Power/connectivity: no authoritative booking-specific source found; marked unverified. Reasonable inference: owners need quick re-sync and mistake-proof double-booking protection (collision-safe booking already helps).
- Languages: francophone (French) is explicit in vendor content (FCFA pricing); Swahili, Yoruba, Hausa, Pidgin support in global tools: none found (unverified). SimplyBook is multilingual in general; African-language coverage unverified.
- Phone/name formats: no source; practical points: E.164 with country picker (+234, +254, +233, +27, +225), accept leading 0 national format, single "full name" field, WhatsApp-capable number.
- Complaints about global tools in Africa (inferred from evidence, not African-user quotes): no local payment rails; USD/EUR pricing; SMS/WhatsApp metering costs (Fresha NGN 2,663 per SMS marketing message); commissions on marketplace clients (Fresha one-time fee; Booksy 30%); Square not available. I did not find Nairaland or African Reddit threads with first-hand reviews (search surfaced none); treat as a gap.

## 5. Differentiators and common complaints (global reviews)
- Fresha (Capterra 4.8 per aggregator): fees ("20% is quite steep"), support slow or gated behind paid plan, ended free model in 2025 (US prices $19.95 / $14.95 cited by Pabau, a competitor), charges when cancelling appointments (one reviewer).
- Booksy: Boost fee charged even when client no-shows (reviews), price rising after reviews accrue, slow support, app crashes, difficulty editing prices (Trustpilot/Capterra summaries).
- Acuity: cost vs. alternatives, confusing tiers/add-ons, clunky reporting, limited booking-page customization without CSS, recurring-appointment and complex-rule setup issues, some cash-based practices could not charge upfront (Capterra/GetApp summaries).
- Setmore: reviewers miss messaging, deposit handling, streamlined onboarding, classes/memberships/POS depth (Zoho comparison page; competitor-biased).
- Booklink: praised for personal support and local ownership; payment setup and calendar sync friction (7 reviews).
- Differentiators that win: marketplace discovery (Fresha, Booksy, Vagaro, YoowiX, Self Vibe), deposits/no-show fees, bundled POS, free tier (Setmore, Fresha formerly), local gateways (Booklink).

## 6. Gaps vs Bookins (context, from the brief)
Bookins has: services (duration, display price, public/private), weekly windows, slot interval, notice, horizon, secure link, guest calendar with tz toggle, .ics/Google add, collision-safe booking, owner list with search/filters/week view/CSV, cancel, derived contacts, Google Calendar connect/clash flags, owner email alert, demo mode. Missing relative to market: guest confirmation (email/WhatsApp), reminders, payments/deposits, reschedule, buffers, time off/holidays, daily caps, staff/resources, intake notes, reviews.

## 7. Top opportunities for an Africa-focused challenger
1. WhatsApp-native flow without paid API: prefilled wa.me confirmation/reminder links the owner taps, plus guest "Add to WhatsApp chat" after booking; later official API with free-window utility replies.
2. Pay-at-venue by default; optional deposit via Paystack/Flutterwave/M-Pesa/MoMo once real flows exist (the AGENTS.md rule: not before proved).
3. Local currency, local-language UI (French first, then Swahili/Hausa/Yoruba/Pidgin), E.164 phone input with country picker.
4. Lightweight, data-cheap, resilient booking page; flat low pricing in local currency with no commission or per-message metering.
5. Cheap table-stakes missing features: reschedule link, buffers, time off/holidays, daily caps, reminders, per-service deposit display.
6. Evidence caveat: African first-hand complaints (Nairaland/Reddit) not found; recommend user interviews with 10 salons/clinics/tutors to validate.
